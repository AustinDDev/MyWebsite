01 / Overview

A clearer view of printing activity.

I implemented vendor-provided DCA monitoring for supported network-connected printers and established a manual Excel tracking process to investigate printing activity and review available maintenance indicators.

The work combined printer-level readings with available workstation-related usage data. Monitoring capabilities depended on the supported device and the data available.

Coverage boundary

Locally connected printers were not covered by DCA monitoring. Any separate readings must be identified by their source; they must not be presented as DCA observations.

02 / Challenge

Investigating unexpected supply consumption.

Printer consumables were being used faster than expected. The organization needed better visibility into printing activity, consumable usage, and printer maintenance indicators.

The investigation needed to distinguish printed-page activity from consumable estimates. Page counts do not directly measure ink or toner consumed by individual workstations.

03 / My approach

Connect the readings to the investigation.

  1. Understand monitoring coverage. Use the vendor-provided DCA for supported network-connected printers and document its limits.
  2. Record readings deliberately. Manually enter relevant readings into Excel, retaining the device context and reading period.
  3. Review usage patterns. Analyze printer-level activity and available workstation-related usage data to identify high-volume printing.
  4. Review maintenance indicators. Examine available fuser page counts and maintenance thresholds alongside usage activity.

04 / Technical implementation

Simple tools. A structured process.

01

Deploy monitoring

Implemented the FLEX Technology vendor-provided DCA for supported network-connected printers. FLEX Technology is the vendor; no separate DCA product name is asserted.

02

Reviewed printer-level and available workstation-related usage information. Availability varied; no universal workstation attribution is claimed.

07 / Results & impact

Operational visibility, grounded in the work.

  • Identified high-volume printing activity.
  • Established structured Excel usage tracking.
  • Improved visibility into supported printer activity.
  • Added visibility into available fuser maintenance indicators.

No verified cost savings, percentage reductions, reduced consumable consumption, or reduction in maintenance incidents are claimed. The fictional demonstration above is not evidence of project outcomes.

08 / Lessons learned

Know the data. Know its boundaries.

Start with reliable readings

Reliable operational data supports better investigations and more informed questions.

Understand the coverage

A monitoring system’s visibility is limited by connectivity, device support, and available information.

Keep the process practical

Simple tools, including Excel, can deliver meaningful operational visibility when readings are organized and reviewed consistently.
